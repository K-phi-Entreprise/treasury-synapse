How the Market view is counted

  • A line means one organisation runs, is implementing, is selecting or has replaced a system for its own finance or treasury, and a public page says so. Every line links to that page.
  • The same sources for every vendor: its public customer stories (the lists on casestudies.com and its own reference pages) one search for its named customers in press releases and conference talks, and one search for job posts that name it. No vendor gets more searches than another. On top come searches that name no vendor: job posts, selection announcements, moves from spreadsheets, and one search per industry, the same for each of the sixteen. People on the map add what their own pages say about their employer.
  • Declared or assumed: a line is declared when the organisation, the vendor, a tender or a filing states it (a case study, a press release, a talk, an annual report). It is assumed when a source only implies it: a job post saying the employer runs the system, or the card of someone who works there. Assumed lines are marked, and every source shows its level. A line with one declared source is declared.
  • Not drawn: a vendor, a consultancy or an integrator as a user; paid customer databases; anything the crawler did not read itself. A job post that only asks for experience with a product draws no line; it is counted on the system's card as a skill asked for.
  • Banks count only when they run a system for their own treasury, never when they use vendor software to serve their clients. The rule is the same for every vendor; it is why vendors that mostly sell to banks look small here.
  • Read the sizes with care: a bubble counts public evidence, not customers. Vendors that publish many customer stories look bigger; systems with a large base and few published stories look smaller. Nothing is weighted or corrected to compensate. This is not market share.
  • Selecting now: a job post or an announcement of the last nine months, unless a later page shows the system running.
  • Industry, size and country: from Wikidata when an organisation of the same name is found, otherwise Unknown. Each organisation's card links to its item.
  • Found at X of N: the denominator is every active large and mid-size company in the French registry (INSEE categories GE and ETI) and every listed company in SEC EDGAR, by filer category. France counts legal units, so a group's French subsidiaries count separately; the US counts listed companies only. A company on the map is matched to the registry by its registry number or its exact name.
  • Public companies: organisations on the map are looked up in the French company registry (INSEE Sirene), the UK register (Companies House) and SEC EDGAR, on an exact name match only. What the registry says (category, headcount, revenue, ticker, industry) is shown with its link and never replaces a correction. Annual reports filed with the SEC (10-K, 20-F, 40-F) are searched for each vendor's distinctive name; a filing counts only when its own words say the filer runs the system, and that line is declared. Vendor names that are ordinary words (Board, Cube, Bound, FIS) are not searched, the same rule for every vendor.
